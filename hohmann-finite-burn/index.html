DEBUGGING AIDHohmann transfer, finite burn and thruster allocation

An independent reference implementation of the sequential pipeline in Sims & Wolek (Lambert, finite burn, pointing, thruster allocation) on the MESSENGER-like layout of Tables 2–3. It exists to check numbers from the students’ own code, not to supply code: compare outputs, column by column. The orbits, time of flight, mass, sensor bore-sight and the thrust bound are all adjustable.

Hohmann / finite-burn debugging reference

1 · Hohmann orbits and time of flight

Start at (r1, 0) on a circular orbit, arrive at (−r2, 0): a 180° coplanar transfer, prograde. Any TOF other than TH is a non-Hohmann Lambert arc.

2 · Sensor bore-sight (body frame)

sB = (sinψ cosφ, sinψ sinφ, cosψ) in the body axes; the LVA thrusts along +b₃. The bore-sight is held on the Earth centre; the roll is the one that puts the LVA axis as close as possible to the commanded thrust at burn 1.

3 · Spacecraft and limits

How to use this. Set the same inputs your code uses and compare the table below, line by line (fuel, Δv, burn times, peak wheel torque, arrival error). Where the paper’s own table has a number, it is shown beneath for reference. Anything here that disagrees with your code is a question to ask, not yet a verdict: the definitions used are listed in Definitions and things to check at the bottom. This is an independent re-implementation, validated against a separate Python/SciPy version and against the paper’s Hohmann row; it is not the authors’ MATLAB code.
Results: from impulsive Hohmann to allocated thrust

Fuel = propellant mass used. Hohmann: rocket equation, Isp 316 s. Finite burn: integrated mass (ideal single thruster, Isp 316 s). Allocation: per-thruster Isp (316 / 235 / 220 s). ‘Wasted’ = (ΣTi − |net|)/|net|, i.e. thrust that cancels. ‘Shortfall’ = |delivered − commanded| / |commanded|, body frame.

Finite-burn solution p = [p1, …, p9]T

Paper ordering: p1 = burn-1 duration t1 (s), p2..4 = T0 (N, inertial, constant over burn 1), p5 = coast tc (s), p6 = burn-2 duration t3 (s), p7..9 = Tf (N). Planar case: z components are 0. Compare with your own p* before looking at anything downstream.

Definitions and things to check

Definitions and things to check

Status of this page. Everything here is computed by an independent re-implementation, in the browser, of the sequential approach (Approach A) described in the draft. It reproduces the paper’s Hohmann row and, using the paper’s printed P*, the ACS row of Table 4. It is a checking tool: the numbers are meant to be compared with your own, and a mismatch is a question, not a verdict. The coupled approaches (B, AB) are not implemented here.

Conventions used

  • Geometry. Two-body, planar. Start at (r1, 0) on a circular orbit, arrive at (−r2, 0) on a circular orbit: a 180° transfer. Lambert (0 revolutions, prograde, Izzo’s formulation with the plane normal supplied explicitly because the 180° plane is otherwise undefined) is solved for the chosen TOF.
  • Spacecraft. MESSENGER-like: 17 thrusters (Table 3 positions and directions; Tmax = 667 / 22 / 4.4 N; Isp = 316 / 235 / 220 s), J from Eq. 19, three ideal wheels along b1, b2, b3. The LVA thrusts along +b3. ‘ACS only’ removes the LVA; ‘LVA+ACS’ keeps all 17.
  • Stage 2 (finite burn). Burn–coast–burn, constant inertial thrust vector in each burn, ideal single thruster with mass flow |T|/(Ispg) at 316 s. The cost is p1|T0| + p6|Tf|. The thrust bound is on the norm, and it is active in both burns, so the optimum is found by solving the four boundary conditions for the two burn durations and the two thrust directions. This agrees with a general SQP solution (checked) and is not the same as the per-component box |pi| ≤ Tmax of Eq. 27.
  • Pointing. The bore-sight is held on the Earth centre with no roll about it. In this planar problem that makes the body rotate rigidly with the line of sight. The initial roll is the one that puts the LVA axis closest to the commanded thrust at burn 1. τpointing = Jα + ω×Jω from Eqs. 52–95.
  • Stage 3 (allocation). At each instant solve min ½|AβTmax − TB|² + ½Σλiβi² with 0 ≤ β ≤ 1 and λi = λ Tmax,i², λ = 10−6 (Eq. 41, read as a penalty on thrust in newtons). Wheels supply τpointing − τthrusters. The delivered thrust is then re-propagated with the actual mass flow, and the arrival error is measured against the target.
  • Wasted thrust = (ΣTi − |net thrust|)/|net thrust|. Shortfall = |delivered − commanded|/|commanded| in the body frame. Fuel for the allocation rows is ∫ΣTi/(Isp,ig) dt. Δv for finite-burn and allocation rows is ∫|net thrust|/m dt.
  • Thrust bound for stage 2. The finite burn needs a Tmax, and it depends on the allocation. ‘Capability’ is the largest net force along +b3 that the allocation can give with no lateral force, computed from the layout: 759.6 N for LVA+ACS and 92.6 N for ACS only (4 × 22 N from C1–C4 plus 4.6 N from the canted attitude thrusters). The paper uses 667 N and tries 22 N; both are selectable.

What the numbers say for the paper’s example (r1 = 7000, r2 = 7250 km, TOF = TH)

  • Agreement. Hohmann Δv 131.236 m/s and fuel 37.19 kg (paper 37.195) with m0 = 897 kg; burn times 88.1 / 85.5 s against 88.21 / 85.04 s; with the paper’s printed P*, the ACS allocation gives 8.08 kg, 17.87 m/s and a peak wheel torque of 4.315 N·m (paper 8.0867, 17.839, 4.3142).
  • Mass. Table 2 gives 1107 kg, but Tables 4–5 correspond to about 897 kg: with 1107 kg the Hohmann fuel is 45.9 kg and the burns last 109 s and 106 s, not 88 and 85. Check which mass the code uses.
  • Bound on the thrust vector. The printed P* has |T0| = 677.6 N, above the 667 N LVA limit, because Eq. 27 bounds each component rather than the norm.
  • Where the LVA+ACS row differs. This tool gives about 40.3 kg (paper 38.014) and an arrival error of about 17 km, because the commanded inertial thrust stays fixed while the body turns with the line of sight (about 5.4° in burn 1), so the sideways demand grows from about 31 N to about 63 N against about 16 N of sideways authority. The paper’s Table 4 reports no arrival error for Approach A. Worth checking in the students’ code: the delivered thrust, not the commanded one, should be propagated, and the arrival error reported.
  • The allocation is weakly determined when saturated. With λ = 10−6, differences between allocations that give the same force cost about 10−4 N², below typical solver tolerances. When the demand cannot be met (ACS only), two exact solvers returned peak wheel torques of 1.78 and 2.15 N·m for the same demand. Compare delivered force, fuel and arrival error rather than individual βi or peak torque in such cases.
  • ACS only: where it fails. It can deliver 92.6 N along the LVA axis but only about 16 N sideways. At the Hohmann TOF the burns (14.7 and 13.5 min) turn the line of sight through about 55°, so a constant inertial thrust vector cannot be delivered: 77% peak shortfall and 143 km arrival error. Away from the Hohmann TOF the problem is harder still: no burn-coast-burn plan was found for TOF more than about +15 s or −17 s from TH (the general SQP check also found none from 10 random starts at +0.5% and +2%; this is evidence, not a proof). The 22 N figure is not the limit; the sideways authority and the rotation of the line of sight are.
  • LVA+ACS: where it works. Arrival error is 16 km at the Hohmann TOF, falls to 0.76 km at TOF = 2989.7 s, and exceeds 100 km beyond about +11 s / −17 s. The bore-sight matters: with ψ = 90° the LVA axis is tangent at the start of burn 1 but the body turns with the line of sight, so ψ slightly above 90° (95°: 1.6 km) partly compensates; ψ = 60° gives 285 km. φ barely changes the arrival error but changes the peak wheel torque (0.75 N·m at φ = 0, 0.57 at 90°).

Not included

Approach B / AB (allocation and wheel limits inside the optimiser), wheel momentum limits, the multi-revolution and non-coplanar cases, and the three further examples of Table 7. Wheel power is not reported because its initial momentum is not specified in the draft.
